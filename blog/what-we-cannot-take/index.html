What We Cannot Take, and Where Each Item Should Go

Not a policy, a permit boundary. Here is the full list of what has to go somewhere other than a junk truck, and the specific place each item belongs.

By Martinez Junk Removal

There is a short list of things we will not put in the truck, and none of it is squeamishness. Hauling regulated waste requires permits and manifests that a general junk removal operation does not hold, and a transfer station that finds it in a load rejects the whole load. So here is the list, plainly, with the right destination for each item — because knowing where it goes is more useful than being told no.

Household hazardous waste

Anything corrosive, flammable, toxic or reactive. In Contra Costa and Solano County this goes to a county household hazardous waste facility, free for residential quantities, appointment-based for most material. Businesses use a separate paid program with different limits.

  • Paint, stain, varnish and thinner — latex paint can sometimes be dried out solid and disposed of as trash, but check your city's rule first
  • Solvents, adhesives, strippers and acetone
  • Pesticides, herbicides, fertilizer and pool chemicals
  • Motor oil, transmission fluid, antifreeze and brake fluid
  • Automotive and marine batteries
  • Propane tanks and camping canisters, including empty ones
  • Fire extinguishers and aerosol cans with contents
  • Mercury thermometers, thermostats and fluorescent tubes
  • Medical sharps, syringes and prescription medication
  • Ammunition, flares, fireworks and anything explosive

Asbestos and lead

Suspect asbestos-containing material — old duct wrap, some floor tile and mastic, certain ceiling textures, transite siding and vermiculite insulation — must be sampled by a lab and abated by a licensed contractor. Disturbing it is a health hazard and an air-district violation. Lead-painted components in pre-1978 homes fall under similar rules for renovation work.

If we find something suspect on a job, we stop, tell you, leave it undisturbed and clear everything around it. That is the honest answer even though it means a smaller invoice. Common on basement cleanouts and attic cleanouts in mid-century Contra Costa housing.

Items we do take, but that carry their own fee

These are legal for us to haul, but the receiving facility charges per item, so they appear as line items rather than disappearing into a volume price. That is transparency, not an upcharge.

Things people are surprised we do take

  • Latex paint that has been fully dried solid, in many jurisdictions — ask us and we will check your city
  • Empty and triple-rinsed containers that once held chemicals
  • Old fuel cans, once genuinely empty and dry
  • Water heaters, furnaces and HVAC air handlers — see appliance and equipment removal
  • Pianos, safes, hot tubs and gun safes, with the right crew and equipment
  • Rodent-damaged and mildewed material, bagged at the source
  • Whole sheds, playsets and above-ground pools, disassembled on site

If you are unsure about an item, photograph the label and text it to (925) 555-0142. It takes us a minute to check and it saves you a trip. What we will never do is quietly bury something regulated in the middle of a load — that risks a rejected load, a fine, and a groundwater problem that outlives all of us.

How to prepare a load so nothing gets left behind

Pull the hazardous items out and put them together in one visible spot — under a sink, in a box by the garage door, wherever. Tell us they are there. We will separate them, tell you which county facility takes them, and in many cases handle the drop-off for you at cost. Everything else goes in the truck, and about two-thirds of it never sees a landfill. The routing behind that is in our county disposal facilities guide.
